# Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 bake-off — 2026-10-25

Purpose: decide whether the debug-only NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 INT8 candidate replaces
Whisper Tiny LID + Canary 180M Flash as Sprich's Automatic recognizer. This is a **public
research-corpus** measurement, not a measurement of the maintainer's voice or of spontaneous
speech in daily conditions.

## Corpus

- **FLEURS test split** (`google/fleurs`, CC-BY-4.0), 50 clips per language for EN/DE/ES/FR,
  fixed by dataset id + source audio path; selections and references are committed in
  [fleurs-test-manifest.json](bakeoff/fleurs-test-manifest.json). Audio is **not** committed.
  FLEURS stores float32 WAV; `scripts/bakeoff-prepare.py` converts to PCM16 16 kHz mono and the
  manifest records both the dataset bytes hash and the exact PCM hash used.
- **20 synthetic silence/noise clips** (10 digital silence, 10 Gaussian noise), generated
  deterministically by the same script; used for blank/hallucination checks and not redistributed.
- **Common Voice English with German-accented speakers: BLOCKED.** The `mozilla-foundation/
  common_voice_17_0` Hugging Face repository exposes no data files without account/terms access
  (dataset-server reports an empty dataset; anonymous file resolution fails), so no metadata or
  clips could be obtained. No substitute was fabricated.

## Method

MEASURED on TCL T807D (Android 16, API 36, API level 36, 8 GB RAM class) with the debug build:

1. Frozen PCM: each clip decoded once by each candidate. Baseline path is production-shaped:
   Whisper Tiny LID per clip, then Canary with the detected language; if LID is unresolved the
   utterance is blank (fail-closed, exactly as production). Parakeet decodes the same PCM with its
   own internal language handling.
2. Latency: decode-to-text wall time per clip (`SystemClock.elapsedRealtime` around decode);
   p50/p95 over 200 speech clips. This is not endpoint→insert: VAD/endpoint is not in the loop for
   frozen PCM.
3. Memory: process PSS before load, after Canary load, after Parakeet load, and at the end.
4. Silence/noise: number of synthetic clips producing non-blank text.
5. Fifteen-minute sustained alternating decode run with per-pair PSS samples.
6. Acoustic subset (20 clips per language from the Mac speaker into the TCL microphone through the
   production IME and a real editor) is measured for the production baseline only; the debug-only
   Parakeet engine has no editor routing in 1.0, so its acoustic numbers are NOT MEASURED.

Normalization (identical for both candidates): Unicode NFKC, lowercase, punctuation removed,
whitespace collapsed; WER over whitespace tokens, CER over characters. No fluency claims.

## Decision rule (fixed before the run)

Parakeet replaces Canary as Automatic only if **all** hold:
overall WER at least 10% relatively lower than Canary; not worse on the acoustic subset;
no single language worse by more than 5% relative; wrong-language rate ≤ Canary's;
warm PSS ≤ 700 MB; p50 decode ≤ 1.2 s; no PSS growth in the fifteen-minute run.
Otherwise Canary remains Automatic and Parakeet stays out of production.

### Acoustic subset (Mac speaker -> TCL microphone -> production IME -> real EditText)

MEASURED for the production baseline (Whisper Tiny LID + Canary), 20 clips per language from the
same committed manifest, played from the Mac speaker at 100% into the TCL microphone through the
release QA build and the QA editor's real EditText. One warm-up capture is discarded before
measurement. Insertion rate **80/80**; WER against the same FLEURS references:

| Language | Clips | Inserted | WER (all) | WER (inserted only) | CER (all) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| EN | 20 | 20 | 0.133 | 0.133 | 0.094 |
| DE | 20 | 20 | 0.080 | 0.080 | 0.048 |
| ES | 20 | 20 | 0.075 | 0.075 | 0.034 |
| FR | 20 | 20 | 0.097 | 0.097 | 0.038 |
| **all** | **80** | **80** | **0.096** | **0.096** | **0.054** |

This is loudspeaker playback into the microphone, not human speech at natural distance; it is not
the maintainer's voice or spontaneous conversation.

Parakeet acoustic subset: **BLOCKED.** The debug-only Parakeet engine has no editor routing in
1.0; adding one would be a production-adjacent feature outside the approved task surface. No
Parakeet acoustic number is claimed. Because the German-accuracy and memory gates already failed
on identical frozen PCM, the missing Parakeet acoustic row cannot change the decision.

The production baseline inserted on all 80 acoustic clips while, on frozen PCM, it blanked 7 of
50 long English clips; the cause of that frozen-PCM blanking remains NOT MEASURED.

### Decision (rule applied mechanically)

- PASS: overall WER at least 10% relatively lower (0.071 vs 0.109).
- **FAIL: German is 45% worse relative (0.096 vs 0.066); the gate allowed at most +5%.**
- PASS: wrong-language rate not above Canary's (both gated by the same Whisper LID; Parakeet has no observable language output).
- **FAIL: warm PSS 1,248,661 KiB ≈ 1,219 MB, above the 700 MB gate.**
- PASS: p50 decode 1,177 ms ≤ 1,200 ms.
- Acoustic criterion: PRODUCTION BASELINE MEASURED (80/80 inserted, WER 0.096); Parakeet acoustic BLOCKED (no editor routing). The decision below rests on the independent German-accuracy and memory failures.
- PASS: 15-minute PSS growth ratio 1.06 ≤ 1.10.

**Parakeet does not become Automatic and stays out of production. Canary 180M Flash remains the
Automatic and Accurate recognizer for 1.0.** The debug-only engine and harness remain for a future
re-evaluation with a larger, human-voice corpus and a memory/latency-qualified device tier.

### Observations outside the decision rule

- Canary produced no text on 7 of 50 long English FLEURS clips (6.6–14.9 s) while Parakeet never
  blanked. The cause is not yet diagnosed; this is a measured baseline quality gap recorded in
  KNOWN_LIMITATIONS, not a reason to ship the heavier model.
- Parakeet's English numbers are substantially better on this corpus, but the German regression,
  the 1.22 GB warm PSS and the larger download outweigh it for a 1.0 release; Automatic must not
  trade one of its four languages for memory risk on a 8 GB tablet, let alone smaller devices.
